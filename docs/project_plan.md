# Project Plan: Fair Speech Recognition Without Accent Labels

**[Your Name] and [Partner Name]**, CS 445: Machine Learning, Fall 2026

**Goal.** Can Group DRO make Whisper fairer across accents using automatically discovered speaker groups instead of accent labels, and how close does it come to using the true labels? We will fine-tune Whisper-base four ways: (1) standard fine-tuning (ERM), (2) Group DRO with true first-language (L1) labels, (3) Group DRO with k-means clusters of speaker embeddings, and (4) Group DRO with random groups. We expect (3) to land between (1) and (2). This is not guaranteed: in Swain et al. (2026), Group DRO with true labels did *worse* than ERM for Whisper on EdAcc. In that case our first result is whether (2) beats (1) at all.

**Connection to the literature.** We adapt the "cluster, then Group DRO" idea of Sohoni et al. (2020; their method is called GEORGE) from image classification to accented speech. We don't use GEORGE as-is: our features come from a pretrained speaker-ID model rather than the task model, and we cluster all speakers together because ASR has no classes. Sagawa et al. (2020) supply the Group DRO training algorithm and its key requirement: on large networks it only helps with strong regularization. Swain et al. (2026) is the closest prior work: true-label Group DRO on Whisper for L2 speakers. Veliche & Fung (2023) and Dheram et al. (2022) show that k-means clusters of speaker embeddings carry fairness-relevant information in ASR, but they used the clusters as input features or for oversampling, not for Group DRO. Koenecke et al. (2020) motivate the problem, and Radford et al. (2023) describe the model.

**Data.** EdAcc, the Edinburgh International Accents of English Corpus (Sanabria et al., 2023), which Swain et al. also used: about 40 h of conversational English from 122 speakers (59 male, 62 female, 1 other), with self-reported L1 and accent labels. Our streaming check found a median of about 12 minutes per speaker (118 speakers have ≥ 5 min) and no speaker shared between the released dev and test splits. We pool both splits and **re-split by speaker** (about 70/15/15), stratified by group and balanced by sex where possible, so test speakers are never heard in training. The labels have a long tail (41 L1 labels, only 17 with ≥ 3 speakers), so for the true-label condition we clean the labels and merge them into broader groups before training, following Swain et al.'s grouping where possible. We chose EdAcc over L2-ARCTIC because L2-ARCTIC would leave only 12 training speakers to cluster (about 2 per cluster at k = 6), too few for k-means to find accent structure.

**Methods.** Group discovery: ECAPA-TDNN speaker embeddings (SpeechBrain), computed per utterance, averaged per speaker, reduced with PCA and clustered with k-means with k equal to the number of true-label groups, plus a sweep over k. Training: Hugging Face Whisper-base (74M parameters), same hyperparameters for all four conditions. Group DRO is the online algorithm of Sagawa et al. (exponentiated-gradient group weights). Whisper-base (74M parameters) can memorize roughly 13k training clips, so we regularize: the primary tool is **early stopping on validation worst-group WER**, because Sagawa et al. found that strong L2 did not help their transformer (BERT) but early stopping did. Strong weight decay is a secondary option. Optional extra: UMAP + silhouette-chosen k, as in GEORGE, since plain k-means can miss small clusters. **Baselines:** ERM (condition 1) and zero-shot Whisper-base.

**Evaluation.** WER (jiwer) on the held-out test speakers, reported per *true* group: average WER, worst-group WER, and the gap between the best and worst group. Mean ± std over 3 seeds, plus bootstrap confidence intervals over test utterances, since many groups have only one or two test speakers. Cluster analysis: adjusted Rand index and NMI of clusters against the true groups and against sex, to explain *why* condition 3 does or doesn't work. Sanity checks: zero-shot WER should be nonzero but reasonable, ERM should beat zero-shot, and we will listen to a sample of the worst transcripts. Answering the question means comparing (3) against (2) and (4); "closes X% of the ERM→true-label gap" is the headline number.

**Compute.** Embeddings and clustering take minutes on a laptop CPU (EdAcc can be streamed, so no local copy is needed). Fine-tuning runs on a local RTX 3070 laptop GPU (8 GB), with free Colab T4s as backup and for running conditions in parallel: 4 conditions × 3 seeds = 12 runs, each expected to take about 1 hour or less. The full dataset is about 7 GB.

**Timeline.**
- *Checkpoint (Dec 3):* data pipeline and splits finished; zero-shot and ERM results; clusters computed with ARI/NMI analysis; first Group DRO (true labels) run.

**Fallback.** If clustering is uninformative (it captures sex rather than accent), we report that as a finding and the project reduces to conditions 1 vs. 2: "Does Group DRO improve accent fairness for small Whisper on EdAcc?" If EdAcc's labels or WER prove too noisy, we run conditions 1 and 2 on L2-ARCTIC (Zhao et al., 2018; 24 speakers reading the same sentences, 6 L1s) as a controlled read-speech test. If Group DRO is unstable, we fall back to the simpler reweighting baseline (upweighting groups by inverse frequency or by loss).
